# Special cases and things that look like a POS but aren't

**Aviv is both.** It's configured as a market POS *and* a restaurant POS. As a market it has the full FTP product/deal sync. As a restaurant it's mapping-and-orders only.

**Airalo** is in the POS type list but isn't a store register — it's a digital eSIM supplier. We pull their packages on a background job and create/update market products from them. Orders are digital.

**Barak** appears in the POS list but is driven by general configuration rather than the market/restaurant flags, so it can look inconsistent next to Comax and All4Shops.

**MarketPrice** shows up inside the POS sync pipeline but is **internal Haat**, not a vendor. Don't include it in vendor-facing docs.

**Unknown** is a placeholder value, not a real system.

# Takeaways for product

1. **Only Tabit and McDonald's can build a menu for us.** Everything else assumes the menu already exists in Haat.
2. **Restaurants need manual mapping work** before any sync or order flow works. That's ops effort per restaurant, and it's the usual source of "the order went to the POS wrong."
3. **Markets need clean barcodes and product codes.** No mapping table means no safety net — a wrong barcode silently means "product not found."
4. **FTP means delay.** Aviv and Barak are batch CSV pulls on a schedule, so their prices are as fresh as the last run, not real-time.
5. **The generic POS API is the strategic path.** New vendors should be onboarded there instead of getting a bespoke integration.
