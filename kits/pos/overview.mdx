# Point of Sale (POS) Integrations — Plain-English Guide

## 1. What is a POS, in one paragraph

A POS ("point of sale") is the software the **business already used before Haat** — the cash register in a restaurant, or the inventory/pricing system in a supermarket. When a business joins Haat, they don't want to maintain their menu twice. So we connect to their POS.

A business is "connected" when it has a **POS certificate** in our database — a record saying *"business #123 uses Tabit, and here are the credentials."*

## 2. Every integration does one or two of these three things

**A. We send orders to them.** Customer orders on Haat → we push the order into their register so the kitchen/cashier sees it. Nearly every POS does this.

**B. They send us catalog info.** Their menu, prices, stock, promotions → we update Haat.

**C. They read our catalog.** Rare — only one POS does this.

When people say "the POS sync," they almost always mean **B**.

## 3. The single most important concept: the matching key

For any sync to work, we need to know *"their item #58821 is our item #4417."* How we do that splits cleanly into two worlds.

**Restaurants → we use `PosId`.** We keep a mapping table (`PosMappings`) where each Haat menu entity (a meal, a deal, a topping, a group) is paired with the POS's own item ID. Someone from ops fills this in from backoffice, or the POS itself creates it during first menu sync. When we send an order, **we translate to their IDs before sending.** We do not send Haat IDs and ask them to figure it out.

**Markets → we use barcode and/or product code.** No mapping table. We look up `Product.Barcode` or `Product.ProductCode` for that specific market. Barcodes are already a shared global language, so no mapping is needed.

That answers the question directly: **we do the mapping, not them.** The one exception is KubeGet (see [Read catalog & orders-only](/kits/pos/read-catalog-orders-only)).
