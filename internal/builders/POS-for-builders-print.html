1. What is a POS?

This guide is for people who build menus in HAAT — Market builders and Restaurant builders.

POS means the system the business already uses in the store.

  • In a restaurant, it is the cash register / kitchen screen (for example Tabit).
  • In a market, it is the system that holds products, barcodes, and prices (for example Comax).

When someone orders on HAAT, the order appears on their screen.

The three jobs a POS can do

Not every POS does all three.

JobIn simple wordsAlmost every POS?
Send them the orderCustomer orders on HAAT → their kitchen / cashier sees itYes
Send us catalog infoThey send us menu, prices, or stock. This is “the sync”Yes
Read our catalogThey copy our menu. KubeGet and Kaman do thisNo

What you must get right

Restaurant builderEvery item that can be ordered has a PosId — meal, deal, and ingredient. Tabit and McDonald's often fill this for you.
Market builderCopy the barcode and product code exactly as they are in the POS file. There is no pairing table.
If the number is wrong, the customer can still order on HAAT — but the shop will see the wrong item, or “item not found.”

2. Matching keys

HAAT and the POS each have their own item numbers.

Example: they call a burger #58821. We call it #4417. Sync and orders only work if we know those two are the same item.

Restaurants = PosId

Think of PosId as “their item number,” saved next to our item in HAAT. Every item that can be ordered has a PosId: meal, deal, and ingredient.

When a customer orders, HAAT changes our numbers into their numbers, then sends the order.

If an item is live on HAAT with no PosId, the customer can buy it, and the kitchen will not know what it is. Map every meal, deal, and ingredient.

Who fills in PosId?

POSWho puts the number in?
Tabit or McDonald's (first full menu)Automatic — created with the menu
BeecommCloud, LeenCopot, Beecomm, PayPo, PizzaHut, Iron, and similarYou, before go-live
KubeGet, KamanThey do it on their side

Markets = barcode and product code

Markets have no pairing table. We find the product by the code written on it. Copy the value exactly. Do not drop a zero. Do not add a space.

Code you copyUsed for
BarcodeFinding the product (Comax, Aviv)
Product codeFinding the product (All4Shops, Barak) and attaching deals
Deals use product code, not barcode. If the product code on the deal does not match the file, the promotion will not work.

Who does the matching? We do — except KubeGet and Kaman, who match on their side.

Before you finish a build

  • The business has a POS certificate (it is really connected).
  • You have their item list with numbers, not only names or a photo.
  • Restaurants: every item that can be ordered has a PosId — meal, deal, and ingredient.
  • Markets: barcode and product code match the file character by character.
  • You placed a test order and the shop screen shows the same items.
  • When they change items later, you update HAAT the same day.

3. Restaurant builders

Only Tabit and McDonald's can build the HAAT menu for you. For every other restaurant POS, you build the menu first, then you map PosId.

Your steps on every restaurant

  1. Get their item list with IDs, not only names.
  2. Build or check the HAAT menu — same items, names, and prices.
  3. Fill PosId on every item that can be ordered — meal, deal, and ingredient. Skip only if Tabit or McDonald's already created the mapping.
  4. Send a test order. Wrong PosId is the usual reason an order “went to the POS wrong.”
  5. Keep it updated the same day they add or replace items.

Tabit

What it does: First time, builds the whole menu and fills PosId for you. If a menu already exists, only updates in stock / hidden. New items may arrive hidden for you to review. Can copy to franchise branches. Also sends order status.

What you do: Review the auto-built menu and hidden new items (they are not live until you review). Do not change PosId unless there is a duplicate, or the restaurant wants a HAAT-only item.

McDonald's

What it does: Creates and updates the menu and PosId mapping. Stock updates turn items available / unavailable.

What you do: Review the synced menu. If McDonald's marked something out of stock, do not unhide it by hand.

BeecommCloud and LeenCopot

We pull their item list so our copy stays fresh. This does not change the HAAT menu by itself.

What you do: Use that list when you map. New HAAT meals will not appear on their own.

KubeGet and Kaman

These work backwards: they copy our menu and match items on their side.

What you do: Keep the HAAT menu clean. You do not have to map for them.

Orders only — you build everything

Beecomm, PayPo, NetLunch, Elpos, PizzaHut, Presto, Beteavon, Iron

These do not update the catalog. You build the menu by hand and map every PosId before the first live order.

  • PayPo and Beecomm send order status, but not menu updates.
  • Presto gets the order as a file. Mapping work is the same for you.

Aviv restaurants: mapping + orders only. No product file for the restaurant menu. Aviv markets are covered in the market chapter.

Remember

  • Only two POS types can build a restaurant menu: Tabit and McDonald's.
  • Wrong kitchen ticket? Check PosId first. Names can look right while the number is wrong.
  • Hidden Tabit items are waiting for you — they are not live yet.
  • A missing dish may be a stock update. Check the POS type before you “fix” it.

4. Market builders

Markets use barcode and product code. There is no PosId table.

Most market POS types update products that already exist, and they create or update deals. They usually do not add brand-new products. The exception is Comax.

There is no safety net. One extra space or a missing zero on the barcode will not show a red error. The order simply will not match on their side.

Your steps on every market

  1. Which POS is it? Comax, All4Shops, Aviv, Barak — or a fully manual catalog.
  2. Get their file (CSV or JSON). Copy barcode and product code exactly.
  3. Build or align the HAAT catalog. Do not invent extra product codes if this POS only updates existing products.
  4. Fill both codes. Barcode finds the product for Comax and Aviv. Product code finds the product for All4Shops and Barak — and it is what deals use.
  5. Send a test order.

Comax

They can add new products, and update price and availability. We pull deals every day at 05:00 (and you can ask for an extra pull).

Match on: barcode or product code for products. Product code for deals.

What you do: Check codes on anything you add by hand. If a deal does not show, the product code does not match.

All4Shops

They send price and availability only. They cannot create a new product. We pull barcodes at 01:00 (mismatch email) and deals at 05:00.

Match on: product code for everything.

What you do: Create the HAAT product first. Then their price can land on it. Open the mismatch email — those rows will never update until you fix the product code.

Aviv

We download their CSV files. Product and deals: once a day. Price file: every 2 hours.

Match on: barcode for products. Product code for deals.

What you do: Build from their file. Prices are only as new as the last 2-hour file — not live.

Barak

We download their CSV. After we read a file, it is moved to an OLD folder so we do not read it twice.

Match on: product code (ItemCode).

What you do: HAAT ItemCode must match their file. If they say “we already sent the file,” it may already be in OLD. Ask for a new file, or a pull from backoffice.

Remember

  • Copy codes exactly.
  • Only Comax is expected to add new SKUs. All4Shops will never create a product for you.
  • Deals use product code, even when the product itself matches on barcode.
  • Aviv and Barak are not real-time.
  • Read the All4Shops mismatch email.

6. How we work together

Sales / Account Manager owns the partner. They chase the client, book calls, and keep the process moving. Product and Content do not own that relationship.

Commercial (SE / AM)

  • Talk to the partner and book meetings
  • Make sure the client answers
  • Set expectations on time and what is included

Content (builders)

  • Collect the files (JSON or CSV) with real IDs
  • Build the menu with the correct PosId (restaurants) or barcode / product code (markets)
  • Update those values when the POS changes items
  • Place a test order and confirm it works
  • Help when an order fails; send real tech bugs to Product / R&D

Product / R&D

  • Join after Commercial has checked the request is worth doing
  • Size the work and decide a small first version vs full integration
  • Technical kickoff; build or turn on the connection
  • They do not replace Commercial as the partner contact

Before Product starts

Commercial answers: market or restaurant? Do they have their own tech team?

About priority

  • Total number of businesses using the POS
  • Number of existing HAAT partners using this POS
  • Presence of enterprise clients (for example franchises, chains, large retailers)

After it is approved

  1. Name the people (one POS contact, one HAAT contact).
  2. Kickoff: what is in the first version, and the dates.
  3. Content gets the catalog file with stable IDs.
  4. Pilot shops go live. Success = test orders look correct on their screen.
  5. After launch: order mistakes → Content first. Broken connection → Product / R&D.
